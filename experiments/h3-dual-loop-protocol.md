# H3 Protocol: Dual-Loop Architecture Separates Evidence Production from Meaning Production

## Hypothesis

The `/autoresearch` dual-loop architecture works because it separates two different cognitive operations:

1. **Evidence production** — generating observations, measurements, protocols, experiment results, and local analyses.
2. **Meaning production** — synthesizing patterns, revising hypotheses, deciding whether to deepen/broaden/pivot/conclude, and forming a narrative.

## Mechanism

A single-loop research workflow tends to either over-optimize local metrics without understanding or over-reflect without generating enough evidence. The inner/outer loop split gives each mode its own rhythm.

- Inner loop: fast, local, operational, metric-oriented.
- Outer loop: slower, global, interpretive, direction-oriented.

## Prediction

A mapping of `/autoresearch` should show that inner-loop activities mainly produce evidence artifacts, while outer-loop activities mainly update synthesis and direction artifacts.

## Evaluation Method

1. Map inner-loop steps to artifacts and outputs.
2. Map outer-loop steps to artifacts and outputs.
3. Compare dual-loop architecture to single-loop alternatives.
4. Identify failure modes when either loop dominates.
5. Derive a reusable design rule.

## Confirmatory Criteria

H3 is supported if the dual-loop model explains:

- why experiments alone are insufficient;
- why reflection alone is insufficient;
- why `findings.md` must be periodically updated;
- why pivot/deepen/broaden/conclude decisions belong to the outer loop;
- why human-facing reports are generated after synthesis rather than after every minor action.
